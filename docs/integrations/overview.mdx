Everything the console and CLI do runs over the [REST API](/reference/api/overview), so a platform can do it too: provision infrastructure for its own customers, from its own backend, and never show them an InstaCloud screen. That is the shape this page covers, whether you call it a multi-tenant setup or an integration: your product on top, InstaCloud underneath.

The API is directly available: [mint a token](/reference/api/overview#minting-a-token) on any account and start building. When you are ready to put a real fleet on it, [contact us](#enterprise-plans) for an Enterprise plan with custom restrictions.

## The recommended setup

Your backend keeps one **master token**: an organization token, minted once (`insta tokens create master --org <orgId>`). It is the credential that creates projects and [mints narrower tokens](/reference/api/overview#minting-a-token), never wider than itself, and it never leaves your backend. From there, pick the isolation each customer needs. Two shapes work:

### A project per customer

When a customer signs up, the master token creates a project for them (`POST /orgs/{orgId}/projects`) and mints a **project token** bound to it. Everything that runs for that customer runs under the project token: it can add services, deploy, and read its own logs and metrics, and cannot reach other customers' projects or mint anything.

- Hard isolation: each customer gets their own Postgres, buckets and compute, plus their own branches for previews and agent runs ([branch per task](/agents/branch-per-task)).
- Per-customer metering is built in: `GET /projects/{projectId}/usage` is that customer's bill.

### An app per customer, in one project

When tenants are lighter, keep one project and give every customer an **app** inside it: one compute service per customer (`POST /projects/{projectId}/services`), deployed, scaled, and stopped independently of the rest.

- Cheaper per tenant: the apps can share the project's Postgres and storage through [secret bindings](/reference/api/project), with per-customer separation inside them left to your schema.
- Logs and metrics still read per app (`GET /projects/{projectId}/logs?group=<app>`), but usage is reported per project, so per-customer billing needs your own accounting on top.

Either way, the organization's audit stream (`GET /orgs/{orgId}/events/stream`) gives you one timeline of everything every customer's infrastructure did, and the [API overview](/reference/api/overview#provisioning-end-to-end) walks the provisioning flow, from organization to running deploy, in four curl calls.

## Enterprise plans

Standard plans cap how many services of each type a branch can hold, sized for one team rather than a fleet of tenants, so an app per customer hits the compute cap first and a project per customer eventually meets its own ceilings. For production multi-tenant use, contact us to be upgraded to an **Enterprise plan with custom restrictions**: limits sized to your tenant count, and guardrails where you want them, per project. Tell us what you are building and roughly how many tenants you expect:

## The API design

The full API design is published, split by which token scope can call each endpoint:

- [API overview](/reference/api/overview): authentication, token scopes, errors, and the end-to-end provisioning flow.
- [Account](/reference/api/account), [organization](/reference/api/org) and [project](/reference/api/project) endpoint pages, generated from the platform's own OpenAPI document.
